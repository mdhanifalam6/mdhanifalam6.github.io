JTBD 01 · YouTube small creatorFeeds case study 01

When my new video gets very few views, I want to understand the one thing I should change, so I can make a better next video and keep going.

Functional job

Find what is holding a video back and fix it in the next upload.

Emotional job

Feel the effort is going somewhere; avoid the embarrassment of "no one watches."

Social job

Be seen as a real creator by friends and family; reach the monetization badge.

Push of the situation

"I spent 8 hours on this video and got 37 views. Something is wrong."

Pull of the new solution

"One clear fix, compared with channels my size, in my language."

Anxiety of the new

"Will it say my videos are bad? Is the advice generic?"

Habit of the present

"I watch big YouTubers' growth tips and guess, like everyone else."

Lead with a strength to reduce anxiety, compare with peers (not big creators) so the pull feels believable, and live inside Studio where the habit already is.

JTBD 02 · Google Maps arriving userFeeds case study 02

When I'm 100 metres from an unfamiliar, large place, I want to know exactly which open entrance to go to, so I can arrive on time without calling anyone.

Functional job

Reach a usable entrance fast, on the correct side of the road.

Emotional job

Feel calm and in control, especially at a hospital or when late.

Social job

Not bother the host or customer with "I'm outside, where are you?"

Push of the situation

"Maps says I've arrived, but there's a wall here and the gate is closed."

Pull of the new solution

"Gate 2, on the left, after the petrol pump, with a photo."

Anxiety of the new

"What if the app's gate is closed too? Can I trust it?"

Habit of the present

"I just call the person or ask someone nearby."

Show "last confirmed 2 h ago" to build trust, and use landmark cues because that's how people in India already give directions.

PM toolkit

How I think through a product problem

Three interview-style drills: a guesstimate, a root cause analysis and a North Star metric.

Guesstimate · Opportunity sizing

How many active YouTube creators in India have under 1,000 subscribers?

Top-down
Active internet users in India (IAMAI-Kantar 2024: ~886 M)~886 M
→ of them, YouTube users (public estimates)~470 M
× have uploaded at least one video (assumption 4%)~18.8 M
× uploaded in the last 30 days (assumption 30%)~5.6 M
× under 1,000 subscribers (assumption 85%)~4.8 M
Active sub-1K creators in India≈ 4.5–5 million

Sanity check: 4.8 M creators posting twice a month is ~10 M new videos a month from India's long tail, which is plausible. This is the reach behind case study 01.

Root cause analysis

Watch time on new channels' videos dropped 12% week over week. Why?

Hypothesis tree
CLARIFY

Is the data real? Check the metric definition, logging changes and pipeline delays. Sudden or gradual? No logging change; the drop started on a Tuesday.

EXTERNAL

Season or events? A cricket final or Diwali week lowers viewing for everyone. Ruled out: big channels are flat.

SEGMENT

Slice the drop by platform, app version, country, format and traffic source.

NO

Web and iOS flat. Shorts flat. Search traffic flat.

YES

Android · long videos · Home feed: −31%, starting with that week's app release.

ROOT CAUSE

Likely cause: the release changed Home feed preview autoplay, and small channels lost the previews that earned their clicks. Action: roll back for a holdout group, confirm, then fix the ranking input.

The numbers are illustrative; the method is the point. Clarify, rule out external causes, segment internal ones, then confirm with an experiment.

Case study 01 · YouTube · Creator tools
Case study 01

First 1K Coach: helping YouTube creators under 1,000 subscribers stop guessing

New channels upload into silence. They get almost no views, no explanation why, and many quit before the Partner Program.

Problem

Small creators can't tell why a video failed, so they repeat mistakes and churn.

Solution

A 48-hour video diagnosis card plus a milestone path to 1,000 subscribers.

North Star

% of new channels that publish 10+ videos in their first 90 days.

01ContextProduct discovery

To join the YouTube Partner Program, a channel needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. A brand-new channel gives the algorithm almost no signal about who the video is for, so early uploads often get a handful of views. That cold start is where most new creators give up.

YouTube Studio shows numbers like retention and click-through rate, but it doesn't tell a beginner what those numbers mean or what to do next.

Studio tells a new creator what happened. Nobody tells them why, or what to do next.

    Case study 02 · Google Maps · Navigation
    Case study 02

    The last 100 metres: why Maps users in India still call to ask "which gate?"

    Maps gets you to the street. The final stretch, from "You have arrived" to the actual door, is where riders, drivers and visitors lose time.

    Problem

    Pins land in the middle of a building or colony, not at an entrance you can use.

    Solution

    Entrance-level pins, a landmark cue and a visitor photo for the last 100 m.

    North Star

    Weekly arrivals that end within 30 m of a usable entrance.

    01ContextProduct discovery

    Indian addresses are often described with landmarks ("behind the temple, near the blue petrol pump"). Hospitals, gated colonies, malls and stations have several gates, and only some are open. Google has started using landmarks in Indian addresses, but at the moment of arrival the pin still often points to the centre of the place.

    As a facility Project Manager at sites like King Fahad Medical City, I saw this every day: a big campus has many entrances, and arriving at the wrong one costs 10–15 minutes. Patients, visitors and delivery drivers constantly asked our staff for directions.

    "You have arrived" is where the user's real job begins: finding the way in.

    K

    Primary segment: high-frequency arrivers (riders and drivers). They hit the problem many times a day, and their trips teach the system where real entrances are.

      Problem statement: people arriving at large or gated places in India reach the wrong entrance because Maps marks a place with one central pin, which costs time and forces phone calls.

      PRD 01 · YouTube Studio · First 1K Coach
      Product requirement document

      First 1K Coach

      A coaching layer in YouTube Studio that explains why a video under-performed and gives creators under 1,000 subscribers one clear next step.

      AuthorMd Hanif Alam
      StatusDraft v1.0
      TargetQ1 MVP
      TeamStudio · Analytics · ML
      01Problem

      • Coaching channels with 1,000+ subscribers in v1

      • As a creator losing motivation, I want to see my progress towards 1,000 subscribers so that small wins feel real.
      • As a Hindi-speaking creator, I want advice in my language so that I understand it without guessing.

SELECT cohort_month,
       COUNT(*) AS new_channels,
       AVG(CASE WHEN uploads_90d >= 10 THEN 1.0 ELSE 0 END) AS pct_10_uploads
FROM (
  SELECT c.channel_id,
         DATE_TRUNC('month', c.created_at) AS cohort_month,
         COUNT(v.video_id) AS uploads_90d
  FROM channels c
  LEFT JOIN videos v
    ON v.channel_id = c.channel_id
   AND v.published_at < c.created_at + INTERVAL '90 days'
  WHERE c.created_at >= '2026-01-01' AND c.created_at < '2026-04-01'
  GROUP BY c.channel_id, cohort_month
) t
GROUP BY cohort_month
ORDER BY cohort_month;

      PRD 02 · Google Maps · Arrival View
      Product requirement document

      Arrival View: entrance-level navigation

      Guide users in the last 100 metres to the right, open entrance of large and gated places, using entrance pins, landmark cues and visitor photos.

      AuthorMd Hanif Alam
      StatusDraft v1.0
      TargetQ1 pilot, 10 cities
      TeamNavigation · Places · Contributions
      01Problem

      For large or gated places in India (hospitals, malls, stations, colonies), Maps navigates to one central pin. Users arrive at closed or wrong gates, wander, and call the person they're meeting. Riders and drivers lose time and money on every such trip.

      02Goals & non-goals

      Goals

      • −30% median wandering time at pilot places
      • Model entrances for the top 5,000 large places in 10 cities
      • 80% of shown gates confirmed correct by visitors

      Non-goals

      • Indoor navigation inside buildings (later)
      • Redesigning turn-by-turn navigation
      • Door-level pins for private homes
      03User stories
      • As a delivery rider, I want to be routed to the delivery entrance so that I don't have to call the customer.
      • As a hospital visitor, I want to know which gate leads to the OPD so that I don't walk around the campus.
      • As a local guide, I want to add a gate and its photo so that other people can find it.
      04Requirements
      #RequirementPriority
      R1Places store multiple entrance points with type (main, OPD, delivery, parking) and open hoursP0
      R2Navigation targets the best open entrance for the user's purpose and travel modeP0

      R4Visitor gate photos with automatic face and number-plate blurP1
      R5"Is this gate open?" one-tap check-in after arrivalP1
      R6Entrance suggestions learned from trip endings, sent to human reviewP2
